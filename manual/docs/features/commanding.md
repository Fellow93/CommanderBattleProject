# Commanding a Formation

As a commander you lead one formation of your side: you pick its troops, fight in its ranks and give it orders with the order bar. This page covers taking a formation, the troop selection screen, the troops of each culture and the tools a commander has.

## Taking a formation

- **When you join a side,** you are put in charge of a free formation straight away, of the class you led last if one is free. The troop selection screen opens on top of the formation list, so you can keep that formation or switch to another class. Even if you close the screen, you lead a formation and spawn with it.
- **The general is never given out this way.** To be the general, take him yourself (see [The General](general.md)). If all 8 formations are taken, you stay on the side without a formation, and a message tells you to take the general from the list if he is free.
- **You can switch formations** from the formation list (N) or by picking another class on the troop screen, as long as you haven't spawned yet this round. Your old formation goes back to the AI.
- **The new formation is taken over as it stands,** with its troops and weapon setup, until you pick new ones.
- **When you leave,** the AI leads your formation, with the troops you last picked.

### The formation list (N)

Press **N** to open the formation list. It shows every formation of your side:

- its troops, weapon setup and number of men;
- who leads it, or, for an AI-led formation, what the AI has it doing;
- a troop pick waiting for the next round, marked "(next: ...)";
- the side's total.

Take a free formation from its row. Your own row has a **Troops** button that opens the troop selection screen. The general is the last row.

## The troop selection screen

The screen has three steps. **Back** returns to the previous step, and **Esc** or **Cancel** closes the screen.

1. **Formation class.** A card for each kind of formation your culture fields (horse archers only where it has them, and the General), with how many of them are free. Yours is marked. Picking another class moves you to a free formation of that class and goes on to step 2. A class with no free formation can't be picked. If the server refuses the move, the chat says why.
2. **Weight.** The class's troops, light to heavy, each with the number of men it would field, its armour and speed. Marks show what your formation fields now and what it will field next round.
3. **Weapon setup.** The chosen troops' weapon setups, each with its icon and description. **Confirm** sends your pick.

Taking a formation from the formation list opens the screen at step 2. The General has a single class, so his card goes straight to step 3.

### When a pick takes effect

- **During preparation,** your pick applies at once: you, your soldiers and your formation's size all change for this round.
- **Once the armies have spawned,** the pick waits for the next round. The formation list and the screen show it as "(next: ...)". If you leave before then, the AI still fields your pick next round.

## Troops

Every culture has its own troops. Each kind of formation offers two or three of them, from light to heavy:

- **Light** troops are levies: padded armour or a gambeson, a real helmet, a decent shield and a proper weapon. They are the most numerous.
- **Medium** troops are the common men-at-arms, in mail with a good helmet.
- **Heavy** troops are the culture's elite in its best armour and weapons. They are the fewest.

Each culture has its own character. Vlandia has the heaviest knights, armoured pike and polearm infantry and crossbowmen with pavises, but no bows and no horse archers. The Khuzaits lead with horse archers and have the weakest infantry. The Aserai are a step lighter than Vlandia and Sturgia at every weight, and a little faster. The troop screen shows each troop's armour, speed and weapons.

### Troops and formation size

**The weight sets how many men your formation fields; the weapon setup doesn't.**

- Every formation has a budget: **54 men** for infantry and ranged, **44** for cavalry and horse archers. Mounted formations get fewer, because every rider brings a horse.
- The budget is what a formation fields with troops of average weight for its kind. Heavier troops field fewer men, lighter troops more.
- The troop screen shows the number of men for each choice before you pick.

With Vlandia's usual AI picks, for example, the infantry field 47 Dismounted Knights, 61 Men-at-Arms and 79 Levy Spearmen.

### Weapon setups

Each troop type has a few weapon setups, such as spear and shield, pike, or sword and shield. The first one is the troop's own kit; the others swap some of its weapons. Your soldiers carry the setup you pick, and so do you.

- **Spears, pikes, lances and throwing weapons come with a sidearm,** a sword, axe or mace for close quarters. One-handed swords, axes and maces, and swung two-handed weapons, carry none.
- **Pikes and two-handed weapons come without a shield.** Pikemen keep their pike and brace stances.
- **Javelins throw by default,** on foot and from the saddle.
- **Archers and horse archers keep their sidearm.**

### AI formations

AI-led formations spread over the weights and setups: the first formation of a kind fields the heaviest troops, the next the medium ones, and so on, each with a different setup. Vlandia's infantry, for example, fields heavy spear and shield, medium sword and shield, and light pikes.

## Leading your formation

### The order bar

Give orders with the stock order bar: **F1-F9** for the orders, the **number keys** to pick formations. As a commander, you order your own formation.

- **Each card is named** after its troops and, for a player's formation, its commander, e.g. "Cataphract (Fellow)". An AI formation's card shows only its troops.
- **The selected card glows gold,** and while the bar is open a gold frame rings that formation's icon on the field.

### The order camera

While the order bar is open in third person, the camera rises and pulls back from you, about 3 m up and 7 m back, so you can see your formation and the ground ahead. You keep control of your soldier.

- **The mouse wheel** brings the camera in or out, up to about 15 m up and 35 m back. Your choice is kept for the session.
- The camera stops short of the ground, so looking up or a hill behind you brings it closer instead of under the terrain.
- It falls back when the bar closes. In first person it does nothing.

### Taking over a soldier

When your soldier dies, you can take over one of your own formation's soldiers. About 3 seconds after you fall, the spectator card on one of your soldiers says **Press F to take control**. Press **F**. It never shows on another formation's soldier.

### Cheer with your men

When you cheer (the stock cheer wheel), your formation's AI soldiers cheer with you, each a moment later, so the cheer rolls through the ranks. About one in three also shouts.

- A soldier joins in only if no enemy is within 20 m and he isn't attacking, blocking, aiming, reloading or switching weapons.
- After you cheer, another cheer within 10 seconds gets no echo.
- The general's cheer reaches his riders only.

### The scoreboard

The scoreboard (TAB) has a **Formation** column with the formation each player leads, its weight and its weapon setup. The bot row counts the kills and deaths of the AI formations.

## Known limitations

- **The order bar's stock formation names.** The game names formations by number with its own fixed names, so the bar may call a Vlandian infantry formation by a cavalry or horse archer name. The formation list names each by its kind and number ("Infantry 1", "Infantry 4", "Infantry 6"), and the card names above the bar show the troops.
- **Saved stock perk choices.** When you join a side, the game sends your saved perk choices from other multiplayer modes. Commander Battle's troops reuse the stock class names, so a saved pick can switch your formation to another weapon setup until you pick on the troop screen.
